About Me

Dr. Vishwajeet Kishore Verma is a Postdoctoral Research Fellow at the Center for Transport Research, Department of Civil, Structural and Environmental Engineering, Trinity College Dublin, Ireland.

Prior to joining Trinity College Dublin, he served as a Postdoctoral Research Scientist at the Civil Engineering Department at IIT Bombay, Mumbai. Additionally, he provides freelance technical consultancy services to industrial organizations.

He holds a PhD from Transportation Engineering Group, Department of Civil Engineering, IIT Roorkee, India and a Masters in Transportation Engineering from NIT Karnataka Surathkal. Dr. Verma has industry experience with Mott MacDonald and over two years of involvement in industry-sponsored research projects, including gender auditing, public transport performance evaluation, and urban transport planning. His professional expertise spans the development of Road Development Master Plans, Urban Roads DPRs and FPRs, Travel Demand Modelling, and Bus Transport Systems.

He is a technology enthusiast focused on integrating innovative data solutions to simplify and improve urban transport systems.

Selecting Transit Systems for Indian Cities

Picking up a sole transit mode as a better option for all kinds of Indian cities is an inefficient and ineffective choice. Instead, the selection of transit type should be purely based on understanding city travel dynamics, geographical developments, and horizon year requirements.

Key Considerations:

  • Bus Rapid Transit (BRTS): Ideal for medium-density corridors with flexible routing needs and lower capital constraints.
  • Metro Transit: Suited for high-density, peak-hour urban corridors demanding mass capacity over long distances.
  • Light Rail / Tram: Useful for historical, low-to-medium demand heritage cores requiring low emissions and localized connectivity.

Integrated multi-modal transportation systems—where feeder modes seamlessly connect to primary high-capacity lines—remain the most sustainable solution for expanding urban centers.

Application of Mathematics in Public Transportation

Public Transport service represents the supply provided to meet commuters' travel demand across an urban network. Mathematics serves as the fundamental language through which transit operators plan, analyze, and optimize these operations.

Primary Areas of Application:

  • Demand Forecasting: Four-step travel modeling (Trip Generation, Trip Distribution, Mode Choice, and Traffic Assignment) utilizes matrix algebra, multinomial logit models, and regression analysis.
  • Scheduling & Fleet Allocation: Operations research and linear programming optimize vehicle scheduling and minimize deadheading kilometers.
  • Service Quality Evaluation: Multi-Criteria Decision Making (MCDM) methods like Fuzzy-AHP and GUT matrices prioritize service attributes to improve passenger satisfaction.
Navigating Career Opportunities in Transportation Engineering

Graduating with an advanced degree in Transportation Engineering offers diverse career paths ranging from specialized industrial consultancies to research institutes and public policy bodies.

Core Professional Streams:

  1. Transport Planner: Focuses on city-wide master plans, travel demand modeling, and policy evaluation. Key tools include ArcGIS, PTV Visum, and Google Earth Pro.
  2. Traffic & Safety Engineer: Involves corridor optimization, junction design, signal phasing, and road safety auditing. Key tools include PTV Vissim, Highway Capacity Software, and AutoCAD.
  3. Research & Academia: Academic research positions in universities, research labs (e.g., NATPAC, CRRI), and doctoral/postdoctoral research.
Publishing Research from Master's & Thesis Projects

Transforming a Master's or PhD thesis into a peer-reviewed publication requires a distinct approach compared to thesis submission. While a thesis documents the comprehensive scope of research, a journal article focuses on a concise, impactful research contribution.

Essential Steps:

  • Identify the Specific Contribution: Extract individual core objectives rather than attempting to condense the entire thesis into one paper.
  • Select Appropriate Outlets: Match the scope of your work with SCI/SCIE indexed journals or reputed peer-reviewed conference proceedings.
  • Structure for Readership: Refine literature reviews to focus strictly on research gaps relevant to your chosen methodology and findings.
